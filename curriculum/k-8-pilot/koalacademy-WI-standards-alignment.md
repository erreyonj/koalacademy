# Koalacademy — Wisconsin DPI Music Standards Alignment

Alignment of the Koalacademy curriculum and the One City K-8 pilot against the Wisconsin
Standards for Music, **General Music (MG)** strand.

> **Status: second pass.** Scoped to K-8 — the 9-12 band is out of scope because the school
> ends at grade 8. Flags reflect what is *written or documented* today. This file is the source
> of truth for the alignment chart published at `/syllabi/wi-dpi-standards-alignment`.

## Purpose and sources

The Wisconsin standards excerpt in
[assets/Wisconsin Music Standards.pdf](../assets/Wisconsin%20Music%20Standards.pdf) covers the
General Music content area across four standards — **CREATE (MG1)**, **PERFORM (MG2)**,
**RESPOND (MG3)**, and **CONNECT (MG4)** — organized into grade bands. This program is aligned
against three of them: K-2 (`e`), 3-5 (`i`), and 6-8 (`m`). That is **52 performance
indicators**.

General Music is the correct strand for this program. Wisconsin defines it as formal courses
exploring music in a non-performance setting, explicitly including music technology,
composition, and improvisation — which is what Koalacademy is. The Performance Music (MP)
strand governs bands, choirs, and orchestras; it is not part of the provided excerpt and not
part of this program.

## Alignment methodology

The first pass measured whether a **component was titled after a standard**, and treated
history and culture as missing whenever no lesson was named for them. That is the wrong test
for this program.

Koalacademy teaches cultural, historical, and industry context as **discourse inside the
lesson that raises it**. The context is not a separate unit; it is the conversation the
material demands at the moment it comes up:

- `US.2` covers Timbaland's signature style, so the lesson necessarily covers what audiences
  expected of a Timbaland record in 2001, how "We Need a Resolution" subverted that
  expectation, and how a record went viral before social media existed.
- The grade 8 Vanguard anchor "Forever YHWH" has a choir in the arrangement, so discussing the
  arrangement means discussing Black gospel tradition and sacred vocal texture. Naming a
  separate lesson "the prominence of Black gospel" would be redundant with the listening work
  already scheduled.

So this pass measures **where discourse actually lives**. Threads that arise reliably inside a
lesson or a Vanguard sitting are documented in a **Discourse** block in the curriculum (the
convention is defined in [KOALACADEMY.md](../KOALACADEMY.md)) and flagged `EMBED` here, with
every thread inventoried in the [Connect discourse map](#connect-discourse-map) below.

Two consequences:

1. **CONNECT is a strength, not a hole.** Every 6-8 Connect indicator is met by written
   lessons, and the K-5 Connect indicators are met through documented Vanguard discourse.
2. **Some indicators are deliberately not pursued.** Concert etiquette and the performer and
   audience-role indicators are a poor fit for a non-performance general music program that
   already handles sharing and critique through class presentations. They are flagged `OUT`
   rather than counted as failures.

## Overall alignment

| Band | Indicators | `CORE` | `EMBED` | `DEV` | `OUT` | `GAP` |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| K-2 (e) | 17 | 7 | 2 | 6 | 2 | 0 |
| 3-5 (i) | 17 | 7 | 2 | 6 | 2 | 0 |
| 6-8 (m) | 18 | 15 | 0 | 2 | 1 | 0 |
| **Total** | **52** | **29** | **4** | **14** | **5** | **0** |

Three findings drive everything below.

**Grades 6-8 are covered.** Seventeen of eighteen indicators are delivered by written lessons,
and the eighteenth is etiquette, which is out of scope by choice. Koalacademy's spine — compose
in Koala, analyze records, document rights, present sources, perform live, reflect — maps
almost one-to-one onto CREATE, RESPOND, and CONNECT, and onto PERFORM as Wisconsin scopes it
for General Music.

**K-5 is aligned in intent and partly unbuilt.** The six documented games and the Vanguard
framework carry real coverage, especially for RESPOND and CONNECT. The remaining twelve `DEV`
cells are creation and performance rows that rest on one-line strand emphases in the grade
syllabi, with deliverables, pacing, and per-strand mastery rows still TBD in
[scope-and-sequence.md](scope-and-sequence.md).

**There are no unexplained gaps.** Every indicator is either covered, documented as embedded
discourse, scheduled but unpaced, or deliberately out of scope with a reason.

| Identify and arrange ideas | `MG1.Cr.4.e` identify ideas verbally, aurally, or technologically — `LISTEN` `TIMBRE` `GAMES` `VS` · `CORE` | `MG1.Cr.8.i` identify ideas using notation and description — `NOTATE` `LISTEN` `GAMES` `JOURNAL` · `CORE` | `MG1.Cr.12.m` arrange short pieces with technology per guidelines — `KSN.2` `SONGFORM.5` `AW.4` `AEP.2` · `CORE` |

**Notes.** Composition in Koala is the strongest single alignment in the document: `AW.3` and
`AW.4` require a complete original arrangement built from documented sources, which is
`MG1.Cr.9.m`, `Cr.11.m`, and `Cr.12.m` in one deliverable. The recurring soft spot at every
band is **improvisation**. Rhythmic and textural improvisation exists (`D4.4` note repeat,
`AEP.1` live effect moves, student-set rhythms in Beat Freeze), but no lesson asks a student to
improvise a melody or a harmonic variation over a progression — the explicit demand of
`MG1.Cr.6.i` and `Cr.10.m`.

**Notes.** PERFORM is where the General Music reading of the standard matters most. Wisconsin's
"sing or play an instrument" language is satisfied in a music technology course by pad
performance and live sampling, which `AEP.5`, `PERFORMANCE.2`, and `PERFORMANCE.3` cover in
detail — including rehearsal, recovery from mistakes, and device readiness. What remains thin
is **singing on pitch and acoustic technique**: echo singing appears in the K-2 and 3-5 strand
lines but no repertoire or vocal-technique material exists, which is why `MG2.P.11.m` sits at
`DEV`. The two K-5 performer-and-audience-role cells are `OUT`: sharing and critique are
already handled by share-outs and class presentations, and formal role instruction is not a
priority for this program.

**Notes.** CONNECT is where the revised reading changes the picture most. Every 6-8 indicator is
`CORE`: `US.1` traces sampling from Jamaican dub through the 1988 MPC, the four case studies
carry producer, artist, engineer, and instrumentalist roles alongside the audience and industry
conditions each record landed in, `SRP.2` and `SRP.5` connect music to law and business,
`SONGFORM.1` and `SONGFORM.5` trace form across eras and into classical comparison, and
`AEP.4` places Koala in a technology landscape. `MG4.Cn.12.m` is `CORE` rather than `DEV`
because the roles the course teaches are specific and varied, and the case studies are situated
in the cultures that produced them rather than presented as neutral technique.

The four K-5 cells are `EMBED`: the anchor songs carry this work through the *who made it*,
*where does it come from*, and *what job did they have* lenses in the Vanguard K-5 extension.
Those lenses are documented, but per-song threads can only be written once
[song-list.md](vanguard-songs/song-list.md) locks the K-5 repertoire.

| Where | Indicators | Thread |

| `US.2` | `Cn.9.m` `Cn.12.m` | Timbaland's signature and what audiences expected of it by 2001 (the Jay-Z "Dirt Off Your Shoulder" reaction reads well on screen), how the "We Need a Resolution" sample exceeded that expectation, how a single went viral before social media, and Aaliyah's standing with Black audiences against the industry's "crossover" framing — including what that term assumes |

## Strengths

1. **The 6-8 project spine covers three standards at once.** The `DYS` → `AW` → `AEP` →
   `PERFORMANCE` arc produces a composition (MG1), a live performance with rehearsal and
   critique (MG2), documented analysis and reflection (MG3), and cultural and historical context
   (MG4) from a single body of student work. Standards evidence falls out of the existing
   deliverables without adding assignments.
2. **Technology-based creation is a direct hit.** `MG1.Cr.11.m` and `Cr.12.m` name technology
   and alternative notation explicitly. Koala sequencing, chopping, pad mapping, and step
   editing satisfy these more cleanly than a traditional general music course would.
3. **Analysis and terminology are unusually deep for middle school.** Two full analysis passes
   over a student-chosen playlist (`DYS.3`, `DYS.4`) plus five case studies (`US.2`-`US.5`,
   `SONGFORM.3`, `SONGFORM.4`) exceed what `MG3.R.9.m` and `R.11.m` ask for.
4. **CONNECT is carried by discourse, which is more durable than a history unit.** Because
   context arrives attached to a record students are already studying, it lands as explanation
   rather than as a detour — and it recurs, since the same songs return all year.
5. **Rights and ethics content has no standards equivalent — and that is fine.** `SRP.1`-`SRP.5`
   is not required by any MG indicator, and it strengthens `MG4.Cn.9.m` and `Cn.12.m` while
   standing on its own as enrichment.
6. **The Vanguard Songs framework is the K-5 alignment engine.** One anchor recording revisited
   through whichever strand is active covers most of RESPOND and all of CONNECT in K-5 with no
   new material.

## Areas for further alignment

Ordered by standards impact per unit of work.

### 1. Write the Discourse blocks that are currently only in the teacher's head — `MG4` `EMBED` rows

`US.2` and the grade 8 "Forever YHWH" anchor are documented as the reference pattern. The
remaining case studies and locked Vanguard anchors need the same treatment so the discourse is
transferable to another teacher and defensible to an administrator. This is writing down what is
already taught, not designing new content.

### 2. Convert the K-5 `DEV` cells by finishing scope and sequence

Twelve of the fourteen `DEV` cells are K-5 and share one cause: the per-strand progression rows
and grade deliverables in [scope-and-sequence.md](scope-and-sequence.md) are still TBD. Filling
those tables — end-of-band targets for `BEAT`, `PITCH`, `TIMBRE`, `FORM`, `NOTATE`, `LISTEN`,
and `CREATE` — moves most K-2 and 3-5 creation and performance cells to `CORE` without new
content, because the intent is already documented.

### 3. Lock the K-5 Vanguard repertoire

The four `EMBED` cells in K-5 CONNECT depend on it. Song selection is also the moment to spread
the repertoire across cultures and eras, which is what makes the *where does this come from*
lens work.

### 4. Name improvisation as its own practice — `MG1.Cr.2.e` `Cr.6.i` `Cr.10.m`

Three `DEV` cells, one per band, and the only indicator row that is weak at every level.
Options that fit the existing course: a documented call-and-response improvisation game in
[playbook/games.md](playbook/games.md) for K-5, and an improvisation pass in `AEP.1` where
students play a melodic figure live over their own loop rather than only performing effects.

### 5. Decide the singing and technique stance — `MG2.P.11.m`

Echo singing is named in the K-2 and 3-5 strand lines but no repertoire or vocal-technique
material exists, and 6-8 performance is entirely pad-based. Either commit to a small amount of
documented singing and instrument technique, or state explicitly in the master syllabus that
this program reads "sing or play" as pad and sampler performance. Both are defensible; the
ambiguity is not.

## Out of scope

- **The 9-12 band.** One City ends at grade 8. Koalacademy's original 20-week course was written
  with roughly an 8th-grade floor and would support a high school offering, but no 9-12 syllabus
  exists and building one is not a current priority. If that changes, the `h` indicators can be
  added back as a fourth column.
- **Concert etiquette and formal performer and audience roles** (`MG2.P.4.e`, `P.9.i`;
  `MG3.R.4.e`, `R.8.i`, `R.12.m`). Sharing, critique, and audience expectations for class
  presentations are already covered in `PERFORMANCE.4` and in K-5 share-outs. Formal etiquette
  instruction across a variety of venues belongs to a performance program; adding it here would
  be paperwork rather than teaching. Revisit if a public showcase becomes part of grade 8.
- **Performance Music (MP) strand.** Not part of the provided excerpt and not part of this
  program. If One City adds a performance ensemble, that strand needs its own alignment pass.
- **National Core Arts Standards.** Wisconsin's standards are derived from NCAS (2014), so this
  mapping is a strong starting point for the NCAS sections still marked TBD in the syllabi, but
  the anchor-standard numbering differs and needs its own pass.
- **Grade-level assessment evidence.** This document maps curriculum to indicators. Which
  artifact proves which indicator for a given student, and how that feeds One City report card
  categories, is open in [resources/written-work.md](resources/written-work.md).
