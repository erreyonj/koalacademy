# Game Bank

Classroom games for the K-8 pilot, each written to the same template so new games drop in
cleanly. Strand codes are defined in [scope-and-sequence.md](../scope-and-sequence.md).

Every game is taught with the I do / We do / You do pattern described in
[routines-and-attention.md](routines-and-attention.md): the teacher demonstrates first, the
class runs a guided round together, then students play for real.

## Template

When adding a game, copy this shape:

- **Strand / Grade band / Group size / Space / Materials** — the at-a-glance header table
- **Setup** — what the room and materials look like before play starts
- **How to play** — the rules, in the order students experience them
- **Win condition** — how a round ends
- **I do / We do / You do** — how to teach the game the first time
- **Concept targets** — what the game teaches and what you can assess by watching
- **Variations and ramps** — easier and harder versions
- **Prep TODOs** — anything that must exist before the game can run

## Beat Freeze

| Strand | Grade band | Group size | Space | Materials |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `BEAT` | K-5 | Whole class | Open floor, room to walk a circle | Guitar or clapping; no props |

**Setup:** Clear a walking circle in the room. Students stand spaced along the circle.

**How to play:** The teacher teaches a one- or two-bar rhythm (clapped, counted, or played on
guitar). Students walk the circle stepping on the beat while the rhythm loops. On every rest
in the rhythm, students freeze. Anyone still moving on a rest steps out (or does a quick
reset task) and rejoins the next round.

**Win condition:** Cooperative by default — the class wins the round when everyone freezes
cleanly on the rests. Can be run elimination-style for older grades.

**I do / We do / You do**

1. *I do:* Teacher claps and counts the rhythm alone, exaggerating the freeze on each rest.
2. *We do:* Class claps and counts the rhythm in place, freezing together, before anyone walks.
3. *You do:* Students walk the circle and play the game.

**Concept targets:** Steady beat, rests as intentional silence, internalizing a rhythm well
enough to predict it. Watching who freezes late tells you who is following neighbors instead
of hearing the rest.

**Variations and ramps**

- K-1: one bar, one rest, slow tempo, teacher counts out loud the whole time
- 2-3: two bars, multiple rests, teacher stops counting after the first loop
- 4-5: rests on off-beats; students clap the rhythm while walking; student leaders set the rhythm
- Change movement style (tiptoe, march, sway) to match dynamics or tempo of the rhythm

**Prep TODOs:** Build a small bank of go-to rhythms per grade band (can live in this file as
it grows).

## Timbre Scavenger

| Strand | Grade band | Group size | Space | Materials |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `TIMBRE` | K-3 (works to 5) | Pairs or small teams | Whole classroom | A few planted objects with distinctive timbres |

**Setup:** Before class, hide or place a few key items with strong sound character (something
smooth, something scratchy, something with a short/dry sound, something that rings). The rest
of the room is fair game.

**How to play:** Students are tasked with finding objects that make a given kind of sound —
smooth, scratchy, short, long, ringing, buzzy. Teams search the room, test candidates quietly,
and bring back their best find. Each team demonstrates its object and the class decides
whether it matches the target word. Surprising finds beyond the planted items are celebrated.

**Win condition:** Every team presents a matching sound; optionally award a point for the most
surprising valid find.

**I do / We do / You do**

1. *I do:* Teacher demonstrates two contrasting objects and names their timbres out loud.
2. *We do:* Class tests one shared object together and votes on the best describing word.
3. *You do:* Teams hunt for their assigned timbre word.

**Concept targets:** Timbre vocabulary, listening with intent, describing sound in words.
This is the K-5 seed of the sound-selection judgment used later in sampling (`D4.1`, `US`).

**Variations and ramps**

- K-1: one target word for the whole class, teacher-led testing
- 2-3: different word per team; teams must justify their find in a sentence
- 4-5: two-property targets ("short *and* ringing"); classify finds into a chart afterward
- Reverse mode: teacher plays a hidden object, students guess what and where it is

**Prep TODOs:** Assemble a small kit of plantable objects; keep a running list of timbre words
per grade band.

## Form Detective

| Strand | Grade band | Group size | Space | Materials |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `FORM` | K-5 | Whole class | Standing room in the middle of class | Playback of a song with clear A/B sections, or live guitar |

**Setup:** Students stand in the middle of the room. Agree on a physical signal for each
section — e.g. face the windows for the A section, face the door for the B section.

**How to play:** The teacher plays music that alternates between a repeating A section and a
contrasting B section. When students hear the section change, they turn to the matching
direction. The teacher watches for who turns on the change versus who turns after their
neighbors do.

**Win condition:** Cooperative — the class wins when the turn happens together, on the change,
without watching each other.

**I do / We do / You do**

1. *I do:* Teacher plays both sections, names them A and B, and models the turn on the change.
2. *We do:* Class listens to one full A/B cycle and calls out "change!" together before adding
   the movement.
3. *You do:* Music runs and students turn on their own; teacher stops cueing.

**Concept targets:** Hearing repetition versus contrast, section boundaries, the foundation of
song form (`SONGFORM`). Late or copied turns show who needs more exposure before naming forms.

**Variations and ramps**

- K-1: exaggerated section contrast, teacher cues the first few changes
- 2-3: subtler contrast; add a third gesture for a C section
- 4-5: full AABA tunes; students predict the next section before it arrives, or one student
  DJ picks the track
- Eyes-closed mode to eliminate copying entirely

**Prep TODOs:** Build a short playlist of songs with unmistakable A/B contrast per grade band;
work up one or two live guitar A/B examples.

## Balloon War Trivia

| Strand | Grade band | Group size | Space | Materials |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `LISTEN` + review of any strand | 3-8 | Whole class | Normal seating | Two balloons tied to each chair, question bank, a pin for pops |

**Setup:** Each student has two balloons tied to their chair. The teacher has a bank of music
knowledge questions drawn from recent lessons.

**How to play:** Students take turns receiving a question.

- Answer correctly: the student stays in, nothing changes.
- Don't know it: the student may pass the question to another student of their choice.
  - If the chosen student answers correctly, the *original* student loses a balloon.
  - If the chosen student answers wrong, the *chosen* student loses a balloon.

A student with no balloons is out (they can keep answering to help allies, at teacher
discretion).

**Win condition:** Last student with a balloon standing wins.

**I do / We do / You do**

1. *I do:* Teacher walks through one full turn of each outcome (correct, pass-and-right,
   pass-and-wrong) with imaginary balloons on the board.
2. *We do:* Two practice questions with no balloons at stake.
3. *You do:* Real rounds begin.

**Concept targets:** Retrieval practice on any recent content — theory terms, drum sounds,
song forms, sampling facts. The pass mechanic surfaces who students believe knows the
material, and passes themselves show you exactly which questions the room finds hard.

**Variations and ramps**

- Team mode: balloons belong to table groups instead of individuals (gentler for grade 3-4)
- Steal mode: after a wrong pass, anyone may raise a hand to answer and award the pop
- Comeback rule: a student who is out re-enters by answering a hard bonus question

**Prep TODOs:** Build the question bank, organized by strand/component code and grade band —
this is the main prep dependency. Track it as a growing section in this file or split it into
its own doc once it's big.

## Musical Taboo

| Strand | Grade band | Group size | Space | Materials |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Vocabulary, all strands | 4-8 | Two teams | Normal seating | Card set: music concept + off-limit words per card |

**Setup:** Prepare cards where each card names a music concept or item ("quarter note",
"kick drum", "chorus", "tempo") plus three to five off-limit words the clue-giver cannot say.

**How to play:** Standard Taboo rules. A clue-giver from the active team draws a card and
describes the concept without saying the concept itself or any off-limit word. Their team
guesses within a time limit. An opposing-team monitor watches the card and buzzes any
violation, which forfeits the card. Correct guesses score a point; teams alternate.

**Win condition:** Most points after a set number of rounds.

**I do / We do / You do**

1. *I do:* Teacher gives clues for one card, deliberately stumbles into an off-limit word, and
   shows the buzz-and-forfeit.
2. *We do:* One open round where the whole class guesses and the teacher clue-gives.
3. *You do:* Teams play with student clue-givers.

**Concept targets:** Forces students to describe concepts by their properties rather than
their names — the strongest check that vocabulary is understood, not memorized. Great for
review before assessments.

**Variations and ramps**

- Grade 4-5: fewer off-limit words (one or two), longer timer
- Grade 7-8: cards drawn from `SRP` and `US` content (publishing, clearance, producers)
- Charades mode: no words at all, act or air-play the concept
- Student-authored cards as an assignment — writing good off-limit words is itself review

**Prep TODOs:** Build the card set, organized by unit/strand. Start with one deck per grade
band; student-authored cards can grow it.

## Silent Ball (Musical)

| Strand | Grade band | Group size | Space | Materials |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `BEAT` | 2-8 | Whole class | Standing at desks or a circle | Soft ball, playback or live guitar |

**Setup:** Students stand spread around the room or in a circle. One ball.

**How to play:** Classic silent ball — the class must be completely silent, and students pass
the ball around. A dropped ball or a bad throw puts that student out. The musical layer: music
plays, and passes are only legal on a specified beat. Start with the downbeat; later call
"and of two", "beat four", or other targets. A pass thrown off the target beat counts as a
bad throw.

**Win condition:** Last two or three students in win. Reset and change the target beat.

**I do / We do / You do**

1. *I do:* Teacher bounces or passes the ball to themselves on the downbeat several times,
   counting out loud.
2. *We do:* Class counts the beat out loud while two volunteers pass on the target.
3. *You do:* Silence begins and the game runs, students counting internally.

**Concept targets:** Beat internalization under pressure — students must track the count
silently and act on it. Off-beat targets ("and of _") are a physical introduction to
subdivision and syncopation, feeding directly into `BMT.4` counting and `D4.2` pattern
placement.

**Variations and ramps**

- Grade 2-3: downbeat only, moderate tempo, teacher counts aloud for the first round
- Grade 4-5: beats two, three, or four; tempo changes mid-round
- Grade 6-8: off-beat targets, two balls at once on different beats, or swing feel tracks
- Cooperative mode: class counts how many clean on-beat passes it can chain

**Prep TODOs:** None beyond a soft ball and a few steady, clearly-counted tracks at varied
tempos.

## Adding new games

Keep the template order, tag the strand, and note the grade band honestly — a game that works
in 4th grade often needs a real redesign for K-1, not just a slower tempo. Log half-formed
game ideas in [ideas-inbox.md](../ideas-inbox.md) until they're playtested.
