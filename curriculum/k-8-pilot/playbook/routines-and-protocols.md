# Routines and Protocols

Standing classroom routines for the K-8 pilot. These are the patterns that repeat every class
regardless of content, so students always know what instruction looks like and how to come
back to focus.

## Protocols

- **SAY SOMETHING** — Read a section of material and then turn and talk to a neighbor or table mate to discuss.
- **3-2-1** — Provide 3 responses to x, 2 responses to y, and 1 leftover question about z or anything lesson-related.
- **EQUITY STICKS** — Same as popsicle sticks to call on scholars.
- **JIGSAW** — Split into groups A, B, C, D, etc. and align on a topic, then re-split into groups with one or more members of A–D and teach that aligned idea to everyone. Likely share after.
- **MUSICAL SHARES** — Can implement multiple say somethings, but scholars walk around the room to music and partner with whoever they end up by.
- **MAITRE D** — pending
- **STAND UP, HAND UP, PAIR UP** — pending
- **BACK 2 BACK, FACE 2 FACE** — With a partner, stand back to back and receive a prompt, or review a section of reading/material; on a cue, turn and face the partner and discuss.

## I do / We do / You do

The default instructional pattern for every game and lesson. Nothing is handed to students
before it has been demonstrated and rehearsed together.

1. **I do** — The teacher performs the whole task alone, narrating decisions out loud.
   Exaggerate the parts students get wrong (the freeze on the rest, the off-limit word slip).
2. **We do** — The class does the task together with the teacher, usually a lower-stakes
   version: clapping in place before walking the circle, an open guessing round before teams
   form, practice questions before balloons are on the line.
3. **You do** — Students run it themselves. The teacher stops cueing and starts watching,
   because this is where the informal assessment happens.

### Worked example: teaching Beat Freeze the first time

- *I do:* Clap and count the two-bar rhythm alone, freezing dramatically on each rest.
- *We do:* Everyone claps and counts it in place, freezing together. Nobody walks yet.
- *You do:* Students walk the circle and play. Watch who freezes on the rest versus who
  freezes when their neighbor does.

The same shape applies to written work, instrument handling, and device procedures in 6-8.

## Attention getters

Rehearse these in week one like a game — the routine only works if the response is automatic.

### If you can hear me, clap ___

Teacher: "If you can hear me, clap once." A few students clap. "If you can hear me, clap
twice." More join. Escalate until the room is with you. Musical upgrades once the basic
version is automatic:

- Clap a short rhythm instead of a count; the class echoes it
- Clap the rhythm of a lyric or vocabulary word the class has learned
- For `BEAT` reinforcement, clap the target rhythm from that day's game

### Melody call, class finish, "shh"

The teacher sings or plays (guitar) the first half of a familiar melody. The class sings the
ending, then immediately rests with a "shh" — hand over mouth. The rest is part of the
routine: the class ends the phrase *and* the noise. Rotate the melody as the class learns new
songs so the routine doubles as repertoire review.

### Notes on use

- Pick one primary attention getter per grade band and keep it consistent for the year
- The routine is taught with I do / We do / You do like everything else
- Never talk over a half-attentive room; run the routine again instead
