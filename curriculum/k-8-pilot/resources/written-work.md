# Written Work — Stance and Implementation

## The stance

Written work in this program is **primarily a corrective and progress-tracking measure, not a
core tenet of the course.** The course is built on doing — playing, moving, listening,
making. A worksheet enters the picture when:

1. **Correction:** a game or informal check shows a specific gap (a student can't place
   rests, can't name a key signature), and a targeted worksheet gives structured reps on
   exactly that gap.
2. **Evidence:** the gradebook needs artifacts, and progress needs tracking across a year.
   A small number of written checks, spaced across the year, provide both without turning
   music class into seatwork.

Written work should never be the first way a student meets a concept — concepts arrive
through games, instruments, and listening (I do / We do / You do), and paper follows only
where needed.

## Sourcing

Rather than authoring worksheets from scratch, use published reproducible books — "fill in
the missing beat", "name this key signature", note-naming drills, and music-symbol coloring
pages all exist in teacher-copyable form. Candidates are tracked in
[booklist.md](booklist.md); it's a growing list, and everything on it is unverified until
reviewed in hand.

## By grade band

### K-1

- Written work is essentially coloring and tracing: color music symbols, trace a quarter
  note, circle the loud/quiet picture
- Purpose is symbol familiarity and fine-motor exposure, not assessment
- Grading is participation/completion only

### 2-3

- Match-the-symbol, fill in the missing beat (simple, one measure), circle the higher note
- First real corrective use: a student who can't freeze on rests in Beat Freeze gets a short
  rest-identification page, then returns to the game
- One or two collected pages per quarter is plenty for tracking

### 4-5

- Rhythm completion in 4/4, note naming on the staff, short listening responses tied to the
  Vanguard Song (a sentence or two)
- Introduce self-checking where possible so paper doesn't consume teacher time
- Exit tickets (one question, one minute) start replacing worksheets as the main written
  artifact

### 6-8

- Written work follows the existing Koalacademy structure: playlist analyses (`DYS.3`,
  `DYS.4`), project proposals (`AW.2`), reflections, and listening journals from
  [../vanguard-songs/student-guide.md](../vanguard-songs/student-guide.md)
- Theory worksheets (key signatures, meter) are corrective only, assigned per-student from
  the booklist based on what games and projects reveal

## Feeding the gradebook

**Participation is the largest single share of the grade.** Observation of play carries most
of the weight; written work produces defensible supporting evidence. Participation is earned
in the everyday work of class — showing up ready, doing the Do Now (the ticket into
discussion — see
[../playbook/routines-and-protocols.md](../playbook/routines-and-protocols.md#do-now-the-ticket-in)),
and engaging in games, routines, and lab. The mix, roughly:

| Evidence type | Source | Frequency |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Participation / ensemble skills | Do Now, games, and routines, observed | Continuous (largest weight) |
| Informal checks | Watching the You do phase (who freezes late, who passes in Balloon War) | Weekly |
| Exit tickets | Last block of class | ~Weekly, 4-5 and up |
| Written checks | Booklist worksheets, short and targeted | A few per quarter |
| Artifacts / projects | Listening journals (3-8), Koala projects (6-8) | Per unit |

**Teacher note — honesty about off days.** Because participation is weighted heavily, build a
low-stakes way for students to say when they "don't have it" today. Encourage that honesty
and honor it: a student who names the day and stays in the room quietly is participating, not
opting out. The point is a grade that reflects a real relationship to the work over the year,
not a single flat day — reward the honesty rather than punishing the dip.
