# Written Work — Stance and Implementation

## The stance

The course is built on doing — playing, moving, listening, making. Written work **leans
corrective**: when a game or informal check shows a specific gap, a short worksheet gives
structured reps on that gap. From grade 2 up, a modest amount of written work is **also**
used to grade and measure progress across the year — enough for a defensible gradebook,
without turning music class into seatwork.

Paper usually follows doing. Concepts typically arrive through games, instruments, and
listening (I do / We do / You do); worksheets enter where they help reinforce, check, or
document what students can already do.

A worksheet enters the picture when:

1. **Correction:** a game or informal check shows a specific gap (a student can't place
   rests, can't name a key signature), and a targeted worksheet gives structured reps on
   exactly that gap.
2. **Evidence and progress:** the gradebook needs artifacts, and growth needs tracking.
   A small number of written checks, spaced across the year, provide both.

## Sourcing

Rather than authoring worksheets from scratch, use published reproducible books — "fill in
the missing beat", "name this key signature", note-naming drills, and music-symbol coloring
pages all exist in teacher-copyable form. Candidates are tracked in
[booklist.md](booklist.md); it's a growing list, and everything on it is unverified until
reviewed in hand.

## By grade band

### K-1

- Written work is essentially coloring and tracing: color music symbols, trace a quarter
  note, circle the loud/quiet picture
- Purpose is symbol familiarity and fine-motor exposure, not assessment
- Grading is participation/completion only

### 2-3

- Match-the-symbol, fill in the missing beat (simple, one measure), circle the higher note
- Corrective use when needed: a student who can't freeze on rests in Beat Freeze gets a
  short rest-identification page, then returns to the game
- One or two collected pages per quarter usually covers progress tracking and gradebook
  evidence

### 4-5

- Rhythm completion in 4/4, note naming on the staff, short listening responses tied to the
  Vanguard Song (a sentence or two)
- Introduce self-checking where possible so paper doesn't consume teacher time
- Exit tickets (one question, one minute) become a preferred written artifact alongside
  occasional worksheets

### 6-8

- Written work follows the existing Koalacademy structure: playlist analyses (`DYS.3`,
  `DYS.4`), project proposals (`AW.2`), reflections, and listening journals from
  [../vanguard-songs/student-guide.md](../vanguard-songs/student-guide.md) — these are
  normal graded work
- Theory worksheets (key signatures, meter) lean corrective, often assigned per-student from
  the booklist based on what games and projects reveal

## Feeding the gradebook

Observation of play carries most of the weight; written work still produces defensible
grades. The mix, roughly:

| Evidence type | Source | Frequency |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Participation / ensemble skills | Games and routines, observed | Continuous |
| Informal checks | Watching the You do phase (who freezes late, who passes in Balloon War) | Weekly |
| Exit tickets | Last block of class | ~Weekly, 4-5 and up |
| Written checks | Booklist worksheets — corrective and progress | A few per quarter (2-8) |
| Artifacts / projects | Listening journals (3-8), Koala projects (6-8) | Per unit |
