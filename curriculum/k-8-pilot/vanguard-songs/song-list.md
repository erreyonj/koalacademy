### Grade 8 — "Forever YHWH" (Jordan G. Welch cover)

**Discourse:** Surfaces whenever the arrangement comes up, which in grade 8 is often.

- **The choir.** A gospel choir texture is not a generic "background vocal." It carries a
  lineage in Black sacred music — call and response, congregational singing, a director
  shaping dynamics in real time. Discussing why the choir enters where it does is the natural
  door into that tradition, and it is more useful than a standalone unit on gospel would be.
- **Restraint as a choice.** The arrangement withholds. Students hear restraint as "empty"
  until they are asked what the space is doing, which connects directly to the 8th grade focus
  on restraint versus complexity.
- **Sacred music in a secular classroom.** The song's spiritual subtext is context, not
  doctrine: what the music is *for* in the setting it comes from, described the same way any
  other function would be.
- **Roles.** A cover, a music director, an arranger, and a choir are four different jobs on one
  recording. Useful against `PERFORMANCE.5` career pathways and `SRP` credit conversations.

## Selection worksheet

For each candidate, answer before locking:

1. What does it offer each active strand or component across the year? (A song that only
   serves one lens will wear out.)
2. Is the recording available reliably (streaming, purchased file) for classroom playback?
3. Lyrics check: appropriate for the grade, and cleared with admin if borderline.
4. Sampling check (6-8): is there a usable excerpt for Koala work, and what would rights
   conversations around it look like? (`SRP` tie-in.)
5. Can the teacher still find something new in it in April?
