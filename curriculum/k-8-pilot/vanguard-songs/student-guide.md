## Grades 3-5

### What is a Vanguard Song?

A Vanguard Song is a song our class keeps all year. We don't listen to it once and move on —
we come back to it again and again, and every time we know more music, we hear more inside
it.

### How we use it

Some days we listen for the beat. Some days we listen for the instruments. Some days we
listen for the parts of the song and signal when they change. It's the same song — but you
will not be the same listener.

### Questions we will ask (you don't need to answer them all at once)

- Where is the steady beat? Can you keep it with your body?
- What sounds do you hear? Can you name three?
- When does the song change to a new part? How did you know?
- How does this song feel? What in the *music* makes it feel that way?
- **The big one, asked in fall and again in spring:** What do you hear now that you didn't
  hear before?

## Grades 6-8

### What is a Vanguard Song?

Your grade has three anchor recordings for the year. They are the texts of this course the
way a novel is the text of an English class. Every concept we learn — form, rhythm, harmony,
production, sampling — will eventually be pointed back at these three songs, and your job is
to keep finding what you couldn't hear before.

### Why the same songs all year

Producers and musicians study a small number of records deeply rather than skimming hundreds.
Familiarity is the point: when you know a song this well, you stop hearing *a song* and start
hearing *decisions* — why the drums drop out there, why the hook waits, why less was more.

### Questions that will come back all year

- What is the form? Where are the boundaries, and what marks them?
- What is the rhythmic feel — straight or swung? Where do the drums sit?
- What was *left out*, and what does the space do?
- What would you sample from this record, and what would you owe the artist for it?
- **Fall and spring, same question:** What do you hear now that you didn't hear before?
