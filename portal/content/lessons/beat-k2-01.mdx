## The beat is the thing that does not stop

A beat is the steady pulse underneath a song — the thing your body wants to walk to. Not every sound in the music is a beat. Some sounds sit *on* the beat. Some sit between beats. Some are silences on purpose.

Today we only care about the steady one. If you can walk it, you can hear it.

Play a short stretch. Ask the room: can you pat the pulse on your legs without speeding up or dragging? If the class drifts, that is the diagnostic — they are following neighbors, not the beat.

## Rests are not nothing

A rest is a beat where nobody makes a sound. It still counts. If you keep moving through a rest, you were following the people around you instead of hearing the silence.

That is the whole game.

<Activate title="Beat Freeze">

Clear a walking circle. Teach a one-bar rhythm with **one rest**, slow, and count out loud the whole time.

1. *I do:* Teacher claps and counts the rhythm alone, exaggerating the freeze on the rest.
2. *We do:* Class claps and counts in place, freezing together, before anyone walks.
3. *You do:* Students walk the circle stepping on the beat. On the rest, they freeze.

The class wins the round when everyone freezes cleanly. Watching who freezes late tells you who is following neighbors instead of hearing the rest.

Full rules live in the game bank: Beat Freeze.

</Activate>
