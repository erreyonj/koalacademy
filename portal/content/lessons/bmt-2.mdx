## After major and minor

`BMT.1` gave you two maps: major (bright) and natural minor (heavier). A lot of the music we sample does not sit cleanly on either map. Three shapes show up constantly: the **blues scale**, **Dorian**, and **Phrygian**.

Mr. Erreyon will walk each one on **guitar** first. The keyboard is the picture so you can see the steps.

## The blues scale

A **pentatonic** scale is five notes. Drop the 4th and 7th from a major scale and you have major pentatonic. The **blues scale** is that minor-pentatonic shape plus one extra note — the **flat five** (the blue note). Six notes, not seven.

In C, the blues scale is:

**C, E♭, F, G♭, G, B♭** — then C again.

The flat five is the lean. It is why a line can sound like blues even when the drums are from somewhere else. Guitar: find C, then the three frets that make that crunch. Keyboard: C, then the black-key cluster around F♯ / G♭.

Listen for the guitar sitting in that six-note box — not a full major scale. Same family as **“Stormy Monday”** (T-Bone Walker). The job is not “is this old music.” The job is naming the box.

## Dorian — minor, with a raised sixth

A **mode** is a scale that starts on a different step of a parent scale. Same notes, different home. Dorian is like natural minor with a **raised sixth**. That one note is the color: still minor, but it does not sit as heavy.

D Dorian uses the notes of C major, starting on D:

**D, E, F, G, A, B, C, D**

The B is the raised sixth compared with D natural minor (which would have B♭).

## Phrygian — minor, with a lowered second

Phrygian is like natural minor with a **lowered second**. The half step right above the tonic is the spice — some people hear it as “Spanish,” some as metal, some as a bass line that leans in.

E Phrygian uses the notes of C major, starting on E:

**E, F, G, A, B, C, D, E**

The F is the lowered second compared with E natural minor (which would have F♯).

## Why this matters for samples

When you grab a record, you are grabbing a *box* of notes. Blues-box samples stack with other blues-box sounds. Dorian on D wants friends that share that raised sixth. Phrygian’s flat 2 will clash with a major-key hook unless you mean it.

Producers you will keep meeting — **J Dilla, Kanye West, Dr. Dre** — pick samples that already live in one of these boxes. You do not need to name the mode before you like it. You name it so you can find a second sample that belongs in the same room.

## What you should be able to do after this

1. Say how a blues scale differs from a major scale (five-note family + flat five).
2. Point to the raised sixth in Dorian and the lowered second in Phrygian.
3. Hear the modern Dorian and Phrygian examples and say which color you think you are hearing.
4. Walk one of the three shapes on guitar or the keyboard diagram.
