## Past 4/4

`BMT.4` locked in **4/4**: four beats per bar, quarter note gets one, count **1-2-3-4**. Most pop and hip-hop lives there. Not everything does.

Today: **3/4** and **6/8** — two other time signatures you will hear in ballads, waltzes, soul, reggae, and jazz. Same idea as before: the top number tells you how the bar is sliced; the bottom number tells you what kind of note counts as one beat. The **feel** is what your Do Now was sniffing at — lilt vs rush.

## 3/4

**3/4** means **three** beats per measure, and the **quarter note** gets one beat. Count **1-2-3**, bar line, **1** again.

That missing fourth beat is why 3/4 has a **lilt** — a gentle lean, like the music is rocking side to side instead of marching in fours. Waltzes and many ballads live here.

## 6/8

**6/8** is **compound** meter: **six** eighth-note pulses per bar, often felt in **two** groups of three — **1-2-3 4-5-6**, with accents on **1** and **4**.

On paper the bottom number is **8**, so the eighth note is the unit. In the room you can still count all six — but the **feel** is often more **rushed** than 4/4: the bar moves faster, and players **swing** or lean the middle eighths so it breathes instead of ticking like a machine.

## The four drum sounds

Before Unit 2 drum design, name the four sounds every beat is built from:

| Sound | Job |
| --- | --- |
| **Kick** | Foundation — usually anchors beat **1** (and other strong spots). The low thump you feel in your chest. |
| **Snare** | Backbeat — in 4/4, often beats **2** and **4**. Momentum and snap. |
| **Hi-hat** | Texture — steady **8ths** or **16ths** (or offbeats). Keeps time while kick and snare punch holes. |
| **Tom** | Fill and transition — breaks the loop for a roll into the next section. |

In **3/4**, the kick might land on **1** only; the pattern still **lilts**. In **6/8**, hats often chatter in eighths while the kick marks **1** and **4** — rushed grid, swung feel.

You will place these on pads in Koala soon. Today: hear them and name them.

## What you should be able to do after this

1. Say what **3/4** and **6/8** mean (beats per bar, what note gets the beat).
2. Count **1-2-3** for 3/4 and **1-2-3 4-5-6** for 6/8 while listening.
3. Name **lilt** as the 3/4 feel and **rushed / swung** as the 6/8 feel.
4. Name the four drum sounds — kick, snare, hi-hat, tom — and say each one's job.
