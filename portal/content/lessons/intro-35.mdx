  <Section name="What is Koalacademy?">
    <Slide copy kicker="What is Koalacademy?">

Koalacademy is this music class — and it is a bridge. We learn how music **works** (beat, pitch, form, notation) *and* we start talking about how people **make** music now.

</Slide>
    <Slide copy kicker="What is Koalacademy?">

We will have real conversations about what it takes to be called a musician or an artist. People disagree about that. That is the point.

</Slide>
    <Slide copy kicker="What is Koalacademy?">

The goal of this course is not that we all have the same opinion. The goal is that **you** feel confident you can:

- Understand the music you love
- Bring a musical idea to life with your voice, body, and (later) notation
- Talk about music at a high level for your grade

</Slide>
  </Section>

  <Section name="Your year">
    <Slide copy kicker="Your year">

By the end of this year, you should be able to:

| Goal | By the end of the year you can… |
| --- | --- |
| **Make** | Compose a short pattern with the class and perform it. |
| **Play together** | Keep a beat without watching your neighbor. Clap simple 4/4 rhythms. |
| **Play together** | Hear major vs. minor mood, and hear when a song changes section. |
| **Listen** | Name drum sounds by ear (kick, snare, hat) and describe tone. |
| **Listen** | Keep a Vanguard Song journal — sentences, not just one word. |
| **Respond** | Read and write a simple one-line rhythm. |
| **Connect** | Ask *why* a song feels the way it does, and give shine to classmates who try. |

</Slide>
    <Slide copy kicker="Vanguard Song">

A **Vanguard Song** is a song our class keeps all year. We do not listen once and move on. We come back to it again and again, and every time we know more music, we hear more inside it.

</Slide>
    <Slide copy kicker="Vanguard Song">

Your song is a **Companion** — the same recording middle school uses as one of their Vanguards. You will not analyze it as deeply as they do; you will live with it all year through beat, sound, form, and feeling, and keep a journal about it.

</Slide>

Questions we will ask (you do not have to answer them all at once):

- Where is the steady beat? Can you keep it with your body?
- What sounds do you hear? Can you name three?
- When does the song change to a new part? How did you know?
- How does this song feel? What in the *music* makes it feel that way?
- **The big one, asked in fall and again in spring:** What do you hear now that you didn't hear before?

  <Section name="CREW">
    <Slide copy kicker="CREW">

We do class in a **circle**. That is CREW.

</Slide>
    <Slide copy kicker="CREW">

A circle means everyone can be seen and heard. No front row. No back row. Shared leadership. When something is hard, we talk it through in the circle instead of letting it sit.

</Slide>
    <Slide copy kicker="CREW">

Community commitments — our moral code — point at three jobs:

- Improve **yourself**
- Improve your **relationships**
- Improve your **community**

</Slide>
    <Slide copy kicker="CREW">

In this class:

- I deserve respect, and you all deserve respect (that is the **S** in CRISP)
- We value communication
- I use many **ASL signs and signals** — we will get familiar
- We give **shine and praise** often
- We try and fail *before* we give up, complain, or self-doubt
- **Transitions are KEY.** Clean transitions give you the most free time and set everyone up to succeed

</Slide>
  </Section>

  <Section name="How a lesson runs">
    <Slide copy kicker="How a lesson runs">

Almost everything is taught **I do / We do / You do**. Mr. Erreyon shows it. We try it together. Then you run it.

</Slide>
    <Slide copy kicker="How a lesson runs">

When the room drifts, we use an attention getter — usually **"If you can hear me, clap ___"**. We do not talk over a half-attentive room. We run the routine again.

</Slide>
    <Slide copy kicker="How a lesson runs">

A lot of talk time uses **SAY SOMETHING**: read or hear an idea, then turn and talk to a partner before we share out.

</Slide>

  <Section name="SAY SOMETHING">
    <Slide copy kicker="SAY SOMETHING">

Partner up. Thirty seconds each.

</Slide>
    <Slide copy kicker="SAY SOMETHING">

1. *I do:* Mr. Erreyon models both answers out loud — one song he loves, and one way he will show **Self-Respect** in music class this year (patience with himself when a rhythm is hard, honesty when he does not know yet).

</Slide>
    <Slide copy kicker="SAY SOMETHING">

2. *We do:* The class generates two or three example answers together so nobody is stuck.

</Slide>
    <Slide copy kicker="SAY SOMETHING">

3. *You do:* Each partner shares:

- One song you love (or want us to hear)
- One way you will show **Self-Respect** in this room

</Slide>
    <Slide copy kicker="SAY SOMETHING">

Then two or three pairs shine out to the circle. Write request names on the board. Those songs are now in the mix.
