## What is Koalacademy?

Koalacademy is a curriculum I designed as the **bridge between traditional music theory and modern music production**. We will have many discussions about what is "needed" to be considered a musician and/or artist.

People disagree about that — in this room and in the culture around us. That disagreement is useful.

The goal of this course is that no matter where your opinion lies (or the larger social opinion), **you** will feel confident that you can:

- Understand the music you love
- Bring your musical ideas to life
- Discuss musical ideas and relevance at a high level appropriate to your grade
- **From 6th grade up:** begin creating original works you can use to impact and develop your own community

Theory is in service of making. We have 1:1 iPads with Koala Sampler whenever the day's You-do needs them. Listening and discourse run all year.

## What is music, and where is music right now?

Some of the names and titles we will look at throughout the year:

**Timbaland, Snoh Aalegra, Aaliyah, Jordan G. Welch, Vanessa Carlton, Kanye West, BLACKPINK, Tori Kelly, Rihanna, Bruno Mars, Snarky Puppy, Missy Elliott, Zedd, Disclosure, D'Angelo, Billie Eilish** — and more.

Do not worry: you will also have a way to **request music** you want integrated into class.

In order to fully engage with these selections — and with our favorite music — we have to:

1. **Understand that not everyone has the same musical background, context, or knowledge.** Some of you already know what a scale is. Some of you may not have ever seen a music staff. We have to reach common ground to have the best discussions.
2. **Understand where what is called "Popular Music" is today, and why.** Most of the music that most people in the USA interact with is considered Popular Music. Think: the songs you cannot stop hearing on the radio. Who makes the decisions on what is popular, and why? What about the artists and music that go against that? How does what is considered popular music influence artists in their musical choices? Why do some people stick to a Pop sound — and how and why do others go against it?

## Targets, and Vanguard Songs

By the end of this year, you should be able to:

| Goal | By the end of the year you can… |
| --- | --- |
| **Make** | Sample, design drums, and shape a track in Koala Sampler on 1:1 iPads. |
| **Make** | Build toward an original sampled project — music that can live in your community, not just in this room. |
| **Play together** | Use shared theory vocabulary (notes, scales, form, rhythm) so the room can talk and play in the same language. |
| **Play together** | Perform and present work: playback, critique, and (later) live performance habits. |
| **Listen** | Hear structure, mood, and production choices in records — not just "I like it." |
| **Listen** | Keep a running journal on three Vanguard Songs and return to the same recordings all year. |
| **Respond** | Name what a producer left *out*, and what the space is doing. |
| **Connect** | Discuss sampling history, rights, and artistic intent at grade level. |
| **Connect** | Argue — with evidence from the music — why an artist stuck to a Pop sound or went against it. |

Grade 6 is the first pass: intentional listening, emotional recognition, basic structure. Grade 7 goes after contrast and stylistic identity. Grade 8 is interpretive — restraint vs. complexity, artistic intent, meaning.

### What is a Vanguard Song?

Your grade has **three anchor recordings** for the year. They are the texts of this course the way a novel is the text of an English class. Every concept we learn — form, rhythm, harmony, production, sampling — will eventually be pointed back at these songs. Your job is to keep finding what you could not hear before.

One song is **Primary** — the default song lessons return to, and the one that gets the deepest analysis. The other two are still yours: if Primary is not the source material you want to work from, you can choose one of the other options. Those stay familiar all year; they just do not carry full discourse depth unless the day's lens points there.

Grade 6 Primary also feeds K–2 as their **Companion**. Grade 7 Primary also feeds 3–5. If the pilot reaches a performance, your piece will be Primary (or, for younger grades, a Companion) — material you have already lived with all year.

Producers study a small number of records deeply rather than skimming hundreds. Familiarity is the point: when you know a song this well, you stop hearing *a song* and start hearing *decisions*.

## How we show up — CREW and CRISP

We run class in a **circle**. That is CREW.

Circles create natural equity: everyone can be seen and heard. They foster shared leadership. They are a space for communal wisdom, transparency, and solution-focused problem-solving. The point is belonging — and agency toward getting better.

**Community commitments** are the moral code. They guide three responsibilities:

- Improve one's self
- Improve one's relationships
- Improve one's community

They are also the framework for talking through difficult issues and bringing your best self into the room.

In this class:

- I deserve respect, and you all deserve respect (the **S** in CRISP)
- We value communication
- I use many **ASL signs and signals** — get familiar
- We give **shine and praise** often
- We try and fail *before* we give up, complain, or self-doubt
- **Transitions are KEY.** They give you the most free time and set everyone up for success

### CRISP habits of character

- **Compassion:** I commit to showing love, kindness, respect and understanding to others.
- **Risk:** I commit to trying new and challenging things, even when it is hard.
- **Integrity:** I commit to doing the right thing, even when no one is looking.
- **Self-Respect:** I commit to showing myself love, patience, kindness, and honesty.
- **Persistence:** I commit to showing patience and never giving up, even when things are hard.

### How a lesson runs

Default shape: **I do / We do / You do**. Nothing is handed to you before it has been demonstrated and rehearsed.

Attention: **"If you can hear me, clap ___"** or a melody call you finish, then "shh." One primary getter. We do not talk over a half-attentive room.

Protocols you will see:

- **SAY SOMETHING** — read or hear, then turn and talk
- **3-2-1** — three / two / one leftover question
- **EQUITY STICKS** — random call, not volunteers only
- **JIGSAW** — become the expert, then teach
- **MUSICAL SHARES** — walk the room to music, talk with who you land next to
- **BACK 2 BACK, FACE 2 FACE** — think, then turn and discuss

On production days we collapse warm-up and listening to protect a real work block. Device handout and collection are routines, same as everything else. Student keyboards cannot talk to Koala over MIDI; if you need the teacher keyboard, you schedule time at that station.

<Do title="Requests and commitments">

Circle. Two rounds.

**Round 1 — music requests.** Each scholar names up to three artists or songs they want in the mix this year. We capture three on the board today (more can land later). No roasting the list. Shine is allowed.

1. *I do:* Mr. Erreyon puts three of his own on the board and says *why* each one is useful — not just "I like it."
2. *We do:* The class practices the sentence frame: "I want _____ in class because _____."
3. *You do:* Volunteers (then equity sticks if the room goes quiet). Three names stay on the board.

**Round 2 — CREW commitment.** One sentence each: the CRISP habit you will hold this week, said to the circle. "I commit to Persistence when the sampler does not do what I wanted." Short. Specific. No speeches.

The room is ready for Unit 1 when the board has requests *and* the circle has heard commitments.
