## A sequence is a pattern that plays itself

Last time, a sound landed on a pad. A **sequence** is a short pattern of those hits that loops on its own, so you do not have to tap every hit by hand.

<figure>
  <img src="/assets/ksn-2/koala-sequence-tab-empty.PNG" alt="Koala Sequence tab empty. SEQUENCE is highlighted. Play, record, and 110 BPM sit above eight empty sequence slots and a 4x4 grid of empty pads." />
  <figcaption>Sequence tab. The boxes up top are pattern slots; the pads sit below.</figcaption>
</figure>

**Play** (the triangle) starts the loop. **Record** (the red circle) catches the hits you play. The number is **BPM** — how fast the beat goes.

You need sounds on pads before a sequence is worth anything. Empty pads make a silent pattern.

## GRID: draw a beat

The easiest way in is **GRID** — a row of little squares across the beat. Tap a square to put a hit there. Tap it again to take it off. Filled squares play; empty squares rest.

<figure>
  <img src="/assets/ksn-2/koala-sequence-tab-map-mode-grid.PNG" alt="Koala Sequence tab in Grid map mode. A dropdown lists GRID, KEYBOARD, NOTE REPEAT, and VELOCITY, with GRID selected. A step grid below shows several filled cells." />
  <figcaption>GRID. Tap squares to place hits. This is how you build a beat.</figcaption>
</figure>

There are other views — **KEYBOARD** for playing pitches and **NOTE REPEAT** for fast repeats — but GRID is all you need to make a pattern today.

## Fast and slow, tight and loose

Tap the BPM number to change the speed. **QUANTIZE** is a helper: turn it **on** and Koala nudges your hits onto the beat so the pattern sounds tight. Leave it **off** to keep your exact timing.

<figure>
  <img src="/assets/ksn-2/koala-sequence-tab-quantize-tempo-controls.PNG" alt="Koala Sequence tab with the tempo panel open showing BPM, TAP, BEATS PER BAR set to 4, and QUANTIZE." />
  <figcaption>Speed and QUANTIZE. On = tight to the beat. Off = your timing.</figcaption>
</figure>

Listen for a repeating pattern you can feel. That steady, looping feel is what a sequence is for.

## What you should be able to do after this

1. Say how SEQUENCE is different from SAMPLE.
2. Say what a hit in **GRID** does.
3. Say what **QUANTIZE** on vs. off changes.
4. Keep a steady pattern going.

Koala day. How this runs depends on your class.

**Ready 5th grade — iPad rotation.**
1. *I do:* Mr. Erreyon opens SEQUENCE, taps a few squares in **GRID**, and plays the loop on the projector.
2. *We do:* The class claps along and names Play vs. Record.
3. *You do:* A few students at a time open the **premade** project and tap out **one** short pattern in GRID, then play it back. Everyone else works the keyboard station or the content station. Rotate.

**3rd / 4th grade (and any 5th not on iPads) — keyboards.**
- Watch the projected demo. Then at the **keyboard station**, play a simple repeating 4-beat pattern and keep it steady while a partner counts. That is your sequence, played by hand.

**If the keyboard block will not hold:** split the room — half at keyboards keeping a pattern, half at the **content station** (short excerpt or article about how repetition drives a groove + one notebook line). Swap halfway.

**If instrument time breaks down:** whole class goes to the **content/literacy station**. Read the excerpt and write two sentences: name a song with a pattern you can feel repeat, and say what repeats. No keyboards, no iPads.

Share: one or two volunteers clap or play the pattern they kept.
