  <Section name="Step sequencer">
    <Slide copy kicker="The step sequencer is the zoom-in">

Last time you made a pattern in **GRID**. The **step sequencer** is the close-up editor where you fix **one hit at a time** — make it longer, shorter, louder, or quieter.

</Slide>
    <Slide copy kicker="The step sequencer is the zoom-in">

There are a lot of buttons here. That is normal. **Persistence** means you do the part you know first, then come back to the hard part — you do not quit because the screen looks busy.

</Slide>

  <Section name="Pencil">
    <Slide copy kicker="Pencil">

The **pencil** tool lets you tap to add a hit or tap again to erase it. A hit is a little bar. A short bar is a quick tap; a long bar holds the sound longer.

</Slide>
    <Slide kicker="Pencil" sub="One hit drawn with the pencil. Longer bar = held longer.">
      <figure>
        <img src="/assets/ksn-4/koala-step-seq-main-view-looping-2.PNG" alt="Koala step sequencer with pencil active. A red horizontal bar on a row spans a short length inside a one-bar loop." />
      </figure>
    </Slide>
  </Section>

  <Section name="Velocity">
    <Slide copy kicker="Velocity">

Every hit has a **velocity** — how hard it hits. Same sound, same spot, but softer or louder makes the beat **breathe** instead of sounding like a machine.

</Slide>
    <Slide copy kicker="Velocity">

Tap **VEL**, then tap a hit. Higher is louder; lower is a soft ghost note. Change one, play it back, and listen for the difference.

</Slide>
    <Slide kicker="VEL" sub="VEL. One hit, one number. Change it and listen.">
      <figure>
        <img src="/assets/ksn-4/koala-step-seq-main-view-velocity-tool.PNG" alt="Koala step sequencer with VEL active. A hit shows a tooltip reading VELOCITY 67 percent." />
      </figure>
    </Slide>
    <Slide copy kicker="Velocity">

There is also a **dice** button for chance — a hit that only plays *sometimes*. That one is extra. Only reach for it if the rest is done.

</Slide>

  <Section name="Fix one hit">
    <Slide copy kicker="Fix one hit">

Koala day. How this runs depends on your class.

</Slide>
    <Slide copy kicker="Fix one hit">

**Ready 5th grade — iPad rotation.**

</Slide>
    <Slide copy kicker="Fix one hit">

1. *I do:* Mr. Erreyon opens the step sequencer, draws one hit with the **pencil**, changes one **velocity**, and plays it back on the projector.

</Slide>
    <Slide copy kicker="Fix one hit">

2. *We do:* The class names pencil vs. VEL and points to loud vs. quiet.

</Slide>
    <Slide copy kicker="Fix one hit">

3. *You do:* A few students at a time open the **premade** project, add or fix **one** hit, and change **velocity** on **one** hit. Everyone else works the keyboard station or the content station. Rotate.

</Slide>
    <Slide copy kicker="Fix one hit">

**3rd / 4th grade (and any 5th not on iPads) — keyboards.**

- Watch the projected demo. Then at the **keyboard station**, play a short pattern with **loud and quiet** notes — some accents, some soft. Play it twice: once flat, once with loud and quiet. Hear how the second one breathes.

</Slide>
    <Slide copy kicker="Fix one hit">

**If the keyboard block will not hold:** split the room — half at keyboards playing loud-and-quiet, half at the **content station** (short Rubin excerpt on fixing and revising work + one notebook line). Swap halfway.

</Slide>
    <Slide copy kicker="Fix one hit">

**If instrument time breaks down:** whole class goes to the **content/literacy station**. Read the excerpt and write two sentences: name one thing you would go back and fix in something you made, and how you would fix it. No keyboards, no iPads.

</Slide>
    <Slide copy kicker="Fix one hit">

Share: one or two volunteers play or describe a loud-and-quiet pattern.
