## One sample, the whole song

`US.1` traced the lineage — breaks, MPC, interpolation vs sampling. Today is one record, zoomed in.

**Aaliyah** — *We Need a Resolution* (2001). **Timbaland** production. **Missy Elliott** co-wrote. The beat is not built *around* a sample off to the side. The sample **is** the foundation — looped, pitched, chopped until it carries the whole mood.

That choice took **Risk**. The source is not a dusty soul record. The melody is not obvious. Listeners in 2001 had almost no reference for the sound. It still went everywhere — radio, video rotation, word of mouth. No TikTok. No algorithm. People just kept talking about it.

Your elevator question was the same muscle: face the wall because everyone else does, or stand the way that makes sense to you?

## Timbaland and the expectation of weird

By 2001, a **Timbaland** placement already meant **expect something strange** — glass hits, Indian strings, beatbox air, silence where a kick should be. Artists who worked with him knew the bounce was coming from left field.

Watch an artist hear it for the first time. Then hear a hit that carries the same producer tag.

## We Need a Resolution

Listen to the finished record first. Do not hunt the sample yet. Just notice what the track **feels** built on.

Thin, pitched, percussive — almost vocal, almost not. The hook lives in that loop. Drums and bass complement; they do not replace it.

## The source: Tricks of the Trade

The sample comes from **Tricks of the Trade**, on the *Cruel Intentions* score (2000) — **John Ottman**, not a crate-digger soul 45. **Lisa Donahue** on the vocal line you hear after Timbaland speeds it up.

<YouTubeEmbed
  id="8qcWYiQsXG4"
  title="Tricks of the Trade"
  artist="John Ottman"
/>

Film score. Not funk. Not gospel. That is the **Risk** — dig where other producers are not looking, pitch it until it becomes **yours**, and build a pop record on top.

Non-traditional melody. Pitched-up timbre. No obvious loop point until someone with Timbaland's ear finds it.

Notice:

- The sample **is** the hook texture — not wallpaper under a separate melody.
- **Pitch** changes the meaning. Same recording, different emotional job.
- Structure follows the loop — verse and chorus orbit what the chop allows.

That is production listening: not only *what* was sampled, but *what job* the sample does in the song.

## Why this case still matters

- **Expectation plus surprise:** Timbaland fans expected weird; this was weirder. You can aim for that in your own work — familiar tool, unfamiliar source (`US.1` Koala pads, `KSN.1` import anything).
- **Clearance is real:** a film-score slice on a major single had to be cleared. Integrity from `US.1` is not abstract.
- **Audience:** Aaliyah was already massive with Black audiences; the industry’s old **crossover** language treated white listeners as the default. This record did not need that frame to spread — worth naming because the word still shows up.
- **Durability:** people still argue about this production twenty years later. A signature sound buys that.
