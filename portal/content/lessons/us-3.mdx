## One vocal loop, a whole identity

`US.2` was a strange source turned into a pop foundation — Timbaland, film score, pitched until it barely sounded like the original.

**Kanye West** — *Through the Wire* (2003) is a different case study: a **soul vocal** sampled, **pitch-shifted**, and looped until it **is** the record. One chop carries the hook, the mood, and Kanye’s early producer fingerprint.

The song also has a real-life **Persistence** story. After a car accident in 2002, Kanye recorded with his **jaw wired shut** — rapping through the wire, literally. The Do Now was not decoration. The sample and the session match the same habit: **keep going when the body says stop**.

## Chaka Khan and Through the Fire

**Chaka Khan** — voice of **Rufus**, then a solo career across R&B, funk, and disco. *Through the Fire* (1984) is a power ballad: big melody, big vowels, room to run.

Listen for the **chorus vocal** — the line that climbs. That is the DNA Kanye lifted. Not the drums. Not the bass. The **singer**.

Clearance note from `US.1`: this is **sampling** — the timbre of Chaka’s voice on tape, not a studio re-sing.

## Through the Wire

Kanye pitched the Chaka sample **up** so it sounds chipmunk-bright — almost cartoon until the drums land. That choice is the lesson: **pitch is production**. You are not only borrowing notes; you are borrowing **feel** and then bending it.

The lyrics match the moment — accident, hospital, doubt, still rapping. The sample’s yearning and Kanye’s story lock together.

## Pitch, structure, and impact

- **Pitch-shift:** speeds up the sample’s playback — higher pitch, faster feel, different age on the voice. `BMT.1` intervals still apply; this is the same math turned into a knob.
- **Structure:** verse and hook orbit the loop. When the sample drops out, you feel the hole.
- **Impact:** the brightness of the chop against heavy lyrics is the tension. Funny and serious at once — on purpose.

**Kanye’s early sampling** — soul sources, pitched vocals, pitched-up choirs — showed a generation you could build a **recognizable sound** from one well-chosen record. Producers after him copied the move until it became a language.

## Chaka still carries it

The source artist outlasts the chop. Decades later, Chaka still owns *Through the Fire* live — same song, no pitch shift, full lungs.
