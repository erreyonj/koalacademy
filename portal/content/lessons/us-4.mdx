  <Section name="Follow the chain">
    <Slide copy kicker="Follow the chain">

`US.2` and `US.3` asked what a sample **does** in a record — foundation, pitch, hook. Today asks a different question: **who owns each link**, and what do you owe when you borrow?

</Slide>
    <Slide copy kicker="Follow the chain">

**Kanye West** — *Lord I Need You* (*Donda*, 2021). Gospel-leaning album late in his career: Sunday Service choir, stripped production, personal and public reckoning. The sample is a **prayer**.

</Slide>
    <Slide copy kicker="Follow the chain">

The catch: Ye sampled **Bri Babineaux's cover** of a song **B. Slade** wrote and recorded first. The cover was **unlicensed**. The original artist was **not credited** on release — but **was consulted**, because of **intentionality**.

</Slide>
    <Slide copy kicker="Follow the chain">

Sampling is **borrowing with purpose**. Your Do Now was the same muscle: if you take something that is not yours, who do you answer to — and does it change what you try to do?

</Slide>
    <Slide copy kicker="Follow the chain">

Today's job is not "find the coolest chop." It is **follow the chain** and ask who should be **named**, **paid**, and **heard**.

</Slide>
  </Section>

  <Section name="Intentionality">
    <Slide copy kicker="Intentionality">

**Kevonstage** walks the credits gap: unlicensed cover, original artist missing from the liner notes, still **consulted** because someone meant to do right.

</Slide>
    <Slide kicker="Listen">

    <Slide copy kicker="Intentionality">

**Legal clearance** and **moral credit** are not the same sentence. Reaching out is **Integrity**. Putting a name on the release is **credit**. Students who produce in this room need both.

</Slide>
  </Section>

  <Section name="Chain of ownership">
    <Slide copy kicker="Chain of ownership">

| Link | Role | Credit / clearance lesson |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **B. Slade** | Composition + original performance | Should appear in the chain; was **not** on Kanye's credits initially |
| **Bri Babineaux** | Cover recording sampled | **Unlicensed** use of cover audio — clearance gap |
| **Kanye** | New production / release | **Intentionality** — reached out to Slade even when credits lagged |

</Slide>
    <Slide copy kicker="Chain of ownership">

Sampling a cover **adds a link**. Skipping clearance on Bri does not erase Slade's authorship. `US.1` Integrity still applies: know **which kind of use** you are making, and **give credit where credit is owed**.

</Slide>
