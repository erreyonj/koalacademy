## Week-one snapshot

K–2 windows are **27–28 minutes** (sometimes a 30-minute Thursday). The current
[30-minute assembly](../../../curriculum/k-8-pilot/playbook/lesson-structure.md) —
entry, warm-up, ~8-minute mini-lesson, ~12-minute activity, exit — is too seated and too
single-block for this band.

Existing K–2 portal lessons still assume circle sitting and partner Do Nows:

- [`intro-k2.mdx`](../lessons/intro-k2.mdx) — sit in the circle, partner talk
- [`beat-k2-01.mdx`](../lessons/beat-k2-01.mdx) — same Do Now shape, then Beat Freeze

The playbook already says “for K-1, halve the mini-lesson.” Week one shows that is not
enough. K–2 needs its **own** lesson shape, not a shortened copy of the 30-minute table.

**Design constraint (classroom, not content):** many scholars in this band — especially
1st and 2nd — cannot hold seated instruction for more than about a minute. The period has
to be movement-dense, with seated time as a short reset, not the lesson. Do not put
diagnostic or disability detail into lesson MDX; keep that knowledge for planning only.

## High area of need: K-2 lesson shape

### What fails in the current assembly

| Block | Problem for this room |
| --- | --- |
| Mini-lesson 7–15 | Eight minutes of concept talk exceeds seated attention |
| Activity 15–27 | One long You-do still needs resets; the “halved mini-lesson” still dumps too much into chairs first |
| Partner Do Nows | Circle sit + partner talk steals the first minutes that should be bodies moving |
| CREW circle as a sit | Culture stays; an 8–12 minute sit does not |

### Draft K-2 assembly (document here; playbook rewrite later)

Still **27–30 minutes**. Still **I do / We do / You do**. Still entry + one focus + exit.
No daily listening block — Vanguard day is a movement sitting, not chairs.

| Min (approx.) | Block |
| --- | --- |
| 0–2 | **Entry** — rhythm echo at the door / moving into space. Not seat-and-listen. |
| 2–26 | **3–5 micro-blocks** (~4–6 min each) — one focus for the day, multiple physical formats |
| 26–28/30 | **Exit** — line-up song or freeze-and-go. Do not cut the exit. |

**Micro-block rules**

- Concept lives *inside* the game or Quaver round.
- Teacher demo is standing, **30–60 seconds**, then We do / You do.
- Seated time is a reset (breathe, hands on laps, eyes on Mr. E), not the lesson.
- Formats can rotate: body percussion → freeze/walk → projected Quaver → line-up chant.
- Partner talk, when used, is **standing** and **20–30 seconds**.
- CREW circle remains culture (belonging, turns, shine) — rehearse it standing or in a
  very short sit, not as a middle-school crew period.

If the printed window is 27 minutes, cut a micro-block, not the exit.

## BEAT vs One City drumming class

One City runs a **separate African drumming class**. K–2 general music `BEAT` must not
become a second drumming period. Plenty of African / tribal drums are in the closet;
leave them for drumming class unless that program is already using them with these
scholars.

| Lane | Owns |
| --- | --- |
| **Drumming class** | Djembe / ensemble pulse, call-and-response on drums, West African repertoire and technique |
| **General music `BEAT`** | Body, feet, freeze on rest, found sound, projected Quaver rhythm games, guitar-led echoes |

**Shared vocabulary only:** pulse vs pattern, rest as intentional silence. That is
enough vertical language for later `BMT.4` / `D4` without duplicating the other class.

`TIMBRE` may still *name* drum sounds (smooth, scratchy, ringing). Naming is not
drumming instruction.

## Quaver — standing-decision pivot

[`teaching-notes.md`](../../../curriculum/k-8-pilot/playbook/teaching-notes.md) currently
says K–5 is unplugged-first and device use is never a dependency. Week one contradicts
that for **foundational rhythm attention**, not for Koala Sampler.

**Stance after week one (log into teaching-notes after this recap is locked):**

- Teacher-**projected** Quaver on the TV via **HDMI from the teacher laptop** (works for
  all classes). K–5 still has **no** student devices.
- Use Quaver for short, high-attention rhythm games the current
  [game bank](../../../curriculum/k-8-pilot/playbook/games.md) does not supply: iconic
  notation, echo, freeze, controlled-tempo tracks.
- Quaver Giant Jam sheets in the closet stay **extra**, not the curriculum (see
  [classroom-setup.md](../../../curriculum/k-8-pilot/classroom-setup.md)).
- Quaver does **not** replace Vanguard Songs, CREW / CRISP, or guitar-first demos.

Unplugged-first remains the default for 3–5 and for K–2 work that is not rhythm-game
attention. Quaver is a tool inside micro-blocks, not a new course spine.

## Companion × game bank: “Let’s Dream In The Moonlight”

**Current lock:** Samara Joy — grade 6 Primary and [K-2 Companion]
([song-list.md](../../../curriculum/k-8-pilot/vanguard-songs/song-list.md)).

**Recording facts:** ~2:46; fast double-time swing (~178 BPM felt / ~360 in double-time
counting); 64-bar AABA; jazz vocal + guitar + rhythm section. Lyrics are romantic
(“tell me that you love me,” “let your lips touch mine”).

Companion criteria from
[framework.md](../../../curriculum/k-8-pilot/vanguard-songs/framework.md): clear steady
beat, strong section contrast, distinguishable timbres, lyrics appropriate **and
singable**, enough depth through March.

### Game-by-game fit (original tempo)

| Game | Fit | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Beat Freeze** (K–5; K-1 wants slow, one rest, teacher counts) | Poor | Walking a circle to uptempo swing is a jog, not a pulse game. Jazz phrasing rests are not pedagogical quarter rests. A guitar-slowed 2-bar A-melody excerpt could run the game — that is not this recording. |
| **Form Detective** | Weak–mixed | AABA / bridge contrast exists (“Even though it’s just pretending”), but it is harmonic and lyrical, not the “unmistakable A/B” K-1 needs. At this tempo sections fly past. |
| **Timbre Scavenger** | Off-game | Scavenger is a room hunt. Song could seed timbre words (voice vs guitar vs bass) in a 1-minute listen, not the game itself. |
| **Silent Ball** (grade 2+) | Poor | Needs a clearly counted straight pulse. Prep notes ask for “steady, clearly-counted tracks.” |
| **Balloon War / Taboo** | Out of band | 3–8 / 4–8 only. |

**Other K-2 lenses:** echo singing fails at this tempo and ornamentation; moonlight/dream
mood still works for a feelings word; locomotor beat-keeping does not.

### Viability score

**4 / 10 as K-2 Companion.**

Keep it as **grade 6 Primary**. The miss is the Companion tag, not the 6th-grade pick.
Grade 6 still gets swing, AABA, jazz timbre, vocal agility, and “what do you hear now.”
K-2 games do not. Lyrics are a second strike if this band ever sings or performs the
Companion (performance seed in the framework).

### Replacement ranking (decision open — do not edit song-list until locked)

Do **not** steal “Chicago” (already 3–5 Companion).

1. **Decouple, same tune, slower record** (strongest “keep the song” path) — K-2 hears a
   slow Billie Holiday / Teddy Wilson “Let’s Dream In The Moonlight”; grade 6 keeps
   Samara Joy. Preserves the vertical conversation. Breaks the current “same recording”
   rule on purpose. Still check lyrics for K-2 singing.
2. **From grade 6’s own list — best game fit (leaning preference)** — Avril Lavigne,
   “I’m With You.” Straight 4/4, walkable tempo, obvious verse/chorus for Form Detective,
   guitar-forward for guitar-first teaching. Lyrics are lonely/searching, not
   romantic-physical. Mood is heavier than kindergarten play; still more usable than
   bebop swing.
3. **Theme continuity + guitar** — José Feliciano, “California Dreaming.” Guitar
   ostinato, moderate tempo, “dream” imagery. Weaker chorus contrast than Avril; better
   Beat Freeze than Samara Joy.
4. **True K-2 pulse, outside the list** — Louis Armstrong, “What a Wonderful World”
   (already the diagnostic in `BEAT-K2-01`). Slow, clear pulse, age-appropriate. Weakest
   vertical tie to 6–8.

### Lyric flag: “I’m With You” and “damn”

Every chorus opens with **“It’s a damn cold night”** (several times per play). That is
mild by radio standards and is usually left alone in “clean” / radio edits — chasing a
clean cut that mutes or replaces *damn* is unlikely to pay off.

| Playback option | Worth it? | Tradeoff |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Official “clean” edit** | Usually **no** | Most still keep *damn*; time spent hunting does not fix the line |
| **Instrumental / karaoke backing** | **Yes as a game track**, not as the only Companion | Solves Beat Freeze, Form Detective, Silent Ball without the word; loses echo singing, feelings words from lyrics, and “who is singing” |
| **Full vocal for short LISTEN sittings** | Only with **admin / school language OK** | Keeps the yearlong relationship and performance-seed path; one mild word lands every chorus |

**Practical split if Avril locks:** keep the vocal as the named Companion (grade 6 already
has it); use an instrumental (official karaoke or a reliable stem) for movement / pulse
games; ask One City once whether *damn* is fine for K–2 when vocals are on. If policy
says no and instrumental-only feels too thin for a yearlong Companion, fall back to
Holiday-decouple, Feliciano, or Armstrong.

**Recommendation:** score is low enough to **change the K-2 Companion**. Current lean:
keep Samara Joy for grade 6; move K-2 toward **“I’m With You”** if the *damn* policy
check (or instrumental-for-games split) clears — otherwise Holiday-decouple or the next
rank. Leave the lock open until this recap is confirmed; do not edit song-list yet.
