Vanguard Songs · Grades 6–8

Three records. One Primary.

Each middle-grade class carries three anchors. Lessons default to Primary unless another song fits the day's lens; the other two stay choice alternatives. Grade 6 and 7 Primaries also feed K–2 and 3–5 as Companions.

Status: locked. Grades 6–8 repertoire is locked. Primary songs carry deep analysis; non-primary tracks are lighter recurring options. If the pilot reaches performance, the piece is Primary (or a K–5 Companion).

Selection for 6–8 adds one more test on top of the K–5 criteria: is there a usable excerpt for Koala work, and what would the rights conversation around it look like? An anchor that can't survive a sampling discussion is doing only half the job.

Grade 6 · the lens

Intentional listening — emotion and basic structure.

6·1

“Let's Dream In The Moonlight” — Samara Joy

Grade 6 · locked · Primary · [K-2 Companion]

Primary discourse piece and K–2 Companion: clear feel, section contrast, and enough emotional range to carry a first year of middle-school listening.

6·2

“I'm With You” — Avril Lavigne

Grade 6 · locked · choice alternative

Choice alternative: a bridge that earns itself and a hook that waits — form and contrast when Primary is not the source a scholar wants.

6·3

“Happy” — NAO

Grade 6 · locked · choice alternative

Choice alternative with a clear sample-able moment so the rights unit has something students already love.

Grade 7 · the lens

Structural awareness, contrast, and stylistic identity.

7·1

“Birds of a Feather” — Billie Eilish

Grade 7 · locked · choice alternative

Choice alternative: contemporary production and stylistic identity for scholars who prefer this over Primary.

7·2

“Chicago” — Michael Jackson

Grade 7 · locked · Primary · [3-5 Companion]

Primary and 3–5 Companion: groove, production detail, and stylistic identity — a record students can imitate and fail to imitate, which is where the lesson is.

7·3

“California Dreaming” — José Feliciano

Grade 7 · locked · choice alternative

Choice alternative: arrangement and feel contrast against Primary for comparative listening.

Grade 8 · the lens

Interpretive discourse: restraint vs. complexity, intent, meaning.

8·1

“Forever YHWH” — Jordan G. Welch (cover)

Grade 8 · locked · Primary

Primary: model of mature discourse — arrangement restraint, harmonic pacing, spiritual subtext, performance intention, and production minimalism.

  • Restraint. What was left out, and what does the space do?
  • Pacing. Where does the harmony move, and where does it refuse to?
  • Intent. What is this performance for, and how can you tell?
8·2

“A Thousand Miles” — Vanessa Carlton

Grade 8 · locked · choice alternative

Choice alternative: piano-led arrangement and clear form — contrast against the restraint of Forever YHWH.

8·3

“DO 4 LOVE” — Snoh Aalegra

Grade 8 · locked · choice alternative

Choice alternative: contemporary R&B production and sampling-adjacent texture — rights and artistic intent on a record students already know.

Questions that come back all year

  • What is the form? Where are the boundaries, and what marks them?
  • What is the rhythmic feel — straight or swung? Where do the drums sit?
  • What was left out, and what does the space do?
  • What would you sample from this record, and what would you owe the artist for it?

“What do you hear now that you didn't hear before?”

— asked in the fall, and again in the spring
