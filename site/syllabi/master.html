Status: scaffold. Program-level structure is set; pacing, calendar, and standards alignment fill in once meeting cadence and the school calendar are confirmed.

Program description

A K–8 general music program built on doing: games, movement, singing, listening, and — in the middle grades — music production. K–5 is unplugged-first and organized by concept strands. Grades 6–8 follow the Koalacademy production curriculum, building toward an original sampled project performed live.

The through-line is listening: every grade carries a small set of Vanguard Songs all year and returns to them as skills grow.

Instructional approach

  • Every game, lesson, and procedure is taught I do / We do / You do.
  • Demonstrations are guitar-first, with the classroom piano as visual aid.
  • Theory is spiraled across the year, not front-loaded; classes accelerate on demonstrated prior knowledge.
  • Classes assemble from modular blocks that fit either a 30- or 45-minute period.

Assessment and grading

Assessment leans on observation of play, exit tickets, listening journals, and projects. Written work leans corrective, and from grade 2 up also supplies gradebook evidence and progress checks.

Materials

  • Teacher: guitar; classroom piano (secondary); soft ball; balloons; card and question sets for games; playback system.
  • K–5 students: none beyond classroom supplies — unplugged-first.
  • 6–8 students: iOS devices running Koala Sampler on scheduled device days (rented if needed), plus headphones.

Wisconsin DPI Music Standards

Mapped. All fifty-two K–8 General Music performance indicators are matched to curriculum codes across the three grade bands in the WI DPI standards alignment chart, with each cell flagged CORE, EMBED, DEV, or OUT. The 9–12 band is out of scope, since the school ends at grade 8.

Headline: grades 6–8 address every indicator in their band. K–5 is aligned in intent but largely unpaced — twelve cells wait on scope-and-sequence pacing rather than on new content. Historical and cultural context is delivered as documented discourse inside lessons and Vanguard listening rather than as standalone history units, and concert etiquette is deliberately out of scope for a non-performance program.
