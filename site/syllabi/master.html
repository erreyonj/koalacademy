Program description

A K–8 general music program built on doing: games, movement, singing, listening, and — in the upper grades — music production. The program runs in three bands. K–2 is unplugged-first, organized by concept strands and anchored by the Quaver framework. 3–5 runs age-adapted ports of the Koalacademy lessons. 6–8 follows the full Koalacademy production curriculum, building toward an original sampled project performed live.

The through-line is listening: every grade carries a small set of Vanguard Songs all year and returns to them as skills grow.

Unit structure (6–8)

The Koalacademy course is twenty weeks, five units. Each unit spans roughly four weeks — two weeks per component, five sessions per component — and the two components within a unit pair a concept with its production craft. Case-study and work-week lessons flex so instructors can expand or compress with student progress.

Instructional approach

  • Every class opens with Mindfulness, then Mini Crew, then a Do Now or Movement as the ticket in, and closes with Closing Crew. The Do Now (notebook by default) is the way into discussion; K–2 always runs Movement and never a Do Now. CRISP is named inside Mini Crew or Closing Crew, not run as its own block.
  • Every game, lesson, and procedure is taught I do / We do / You do. Portal lessons follow Do Now → Lesson → Activate, with I do / We do / You do on Activate.
  • Teacher demonstrations are guitar-first, piano as visual aid; the student hands-on instrument is the keyboard, run as a keyboard-station rotation when 1:1 is not possible.
  • Theory is spiraled across the year, not front-loaded; classes accelerate on demonstrated prior knowledge.
  • Classes assemble from modular blocks that fit 30-, 55-, or 60-minute periods. Post-intro, each concept runs a Lesson → Review/Activity → Lab rotation across meetings.

Assessment and grading

Participation is the largest single share of the grade — earned through the Do Now, games, routines, and lab, observed continuously, with a low-stakes way for a student to name an off day rather than be punished for the dip. Beyond participation, assessment leans on observation of play, exit tickets, listening journals, and projects. Written work leans corrective, and from grade 2 up also supplies gradebook evidence and progress checks.

Materials

  • Every student, every band: a notebook — the Do Now and journal surface.
  • Teacher: guitar; classroom piano (secondary); soft ball; balloons; card and question sets for games; playback system.
  • Student hands-on instrument: keyboards, run as a keyboard station when there are not enough units for 1:1.
  • K–2 students: notebook and classroom supplies (unplugged-first; Quaver runs teacher-projected).
  • 6–8 students: 1:1 iPads (30 in the building) with Koala Sampler and headphones on production days. Continued iPad access is an earned privilege gated by file management — Clever sign-in, a per-student folder, and sign-out — and revised sound work goes up on the High Quality Work wall.
  • Books and reproducibles: see the resources catalog.

Standards alignment

Wisconsin DPI Music Standards

All fifty-two K–8 General Music performance indicators are matched to curriculum codes across the three grade bands in the WI DPI standards alignment chart, with each cell flagged CORE, EMBED, DEV, or OUT. The 9–12 band is out of scope, since the school ends at grade 8.

Headline: grades 6–8 address every indicator in their band. K–5 is aligned in intent but largely unpaced — twelve cells wait on scope-and-sequence pacing rather than on new content. Historical and cultural context is delivered as documented discourse inside lessons and Vanguard listening rather than as standalone history units, and concert etiquette is deliberately out of scope for a non-performance program.
