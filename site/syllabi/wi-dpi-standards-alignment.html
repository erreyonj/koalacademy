Wisconsin DPI Standards Alignment

All seventy General Music performance indicators, mapped to Koalacademy lesson codes and K–5 strands — with every remaining gap flagged rather than hidden.

Status: first full pass. Flags describe what is written today, not what is planned for the pilot year. The maintained source of this chart is koalacademy-WI-standards-alignment.md in the curriculum repository.

What is being aligned

The Wisconsin Standards for Music organize music learning around four artistic processes — Create, Perform, Respond, and Connect — across four grade bands: K–2 e, 3–5 i, 6–8 m, and 9–12 h. Seventy performance indicators in total.

This program is aligned against the General Music (MG) strand, which Wisconsin defines as formal courses exploring music in a non-performance setting — explicitly including music technology, composition, and improvisation. The Performance Music strand governs bands, choirs, and orchestras, and is not part of this program.

Koalacademy content assessed here: the fifty written 6–8 lessons, the six documented classroom games, the K–5 strand map, the nine grade syllabi, the Vanguard Songs framework, and the written-work stance.

How to read the chart

Every cell carries the Wisconsin indicator code, a short restatement of the indicator, the Koalacademy codes that deliver it, and exactly one coverage flag.

  • COREWritten material delivers the indicator today — a lesson, a documented game, or a documented framework.
  • DEVPartially covered, or named as intent in a syllabus, with materials and pacing still to build.
  • GAPNo current coverage and no documented plan.

Curriculum codes

  • BMT KSN US D4 SONGFORM SRP DYS AW AEP PERFORMANCE — Koalacademy lesson codes, grades 6–8.
  • BEAT PITCH TIMBRE FORM NOTATE LISTEN CREATE — K–5 strand codes.
  • GAMES — the documented game bank: Beat Freeze, Silent Ball, Timbre Scavenger, Form Detective, Balloon War Trivia, Musical Taboo.
  • VS — Vanguard Songs anchor listening.
  • JOURNAL — listening journals, exit tickets, and written checks.

From 6–8 up, Connect is a natural fit the course reaches almost by accident: US.1 traces sampling from Jamaican dub through the 1988 MPC, SRP.2 and SRP.5 connect music to law and business, SONGFORM.5 compares beat flips to classical movements, and AEP.4 places Koala in a technology landscape. The four K–5 cells are the largest single hole in the alignment.

Where the program is strong

  • The 6–8 project spine covers three standards at once. The DYS → AW → AEP → PERFORMANCE arc yields a composition, a rehearsed live performance, documented analysis and reflection, and cultural context from one body of student work — so standards evidence falls out of existing deliverables.
  • Technology-based creation is a direct hit. Four Create indicators name technology and alternative notation explicitly; Koala sequencing, chopping, pad mapping, and step editing satisfy them more cleanly than a traditional general music course would.
  • Analysis runs deep for middle school. Two full analysis passes over a student-chosen playlist plus five case studies exceed the 6–8 Respond indicators and reach into the 9–12 band.
  • Rights and ethics is enrichment beyond the standards. No MG indicator requires the SRP component, and it still strengthens the claim on MG4.Cn.9.m.
  • Vanguard Songs is the K–5 alignment engine. One anchor recording revisited through the active strand covers most of Respond and half of Connect with no new material.

Areas for further alignment

Ordered by standards impact per unit of work.

  • 1 · Close the four K–5 Connect gaps. Add two documented lenses to the K–5 Vanguard extension — who made this and what was their job, and when and where does this come from — and select repertoire across cultures and eras. Converts four GAP cells without adding a lesson.
  • 2 · Add performer and audience roles to K–5. One recurring “audience job” routine at share-outs, plus etiquette taught before any performance, closes four GAP cells across two standards.
  • 3 · Broaden etiquette beyond the classroom. The indicators ask for a variety of settings. Resolving the open grade-8 public showcase question would close both DEV cells; AEP.5 already produces a set that could travel.
  • 4 · Name improvisation as its own practice. The only indicator row that is weak at every band. A documented call-and-response game for K–5, and an improvisation pass in AEP.1 where students play a live figure over their own loop rather than only performing effects.
  • 5 · Decide the singing and technique stance. Either commit to a documented amount of singing and instrument technique, or state in the master syllabus that this program reads “sing or play” as pad and sampler performance. Both are defensible; the ambiguity is not.
  • 6 · Finish scope and sequence. Twelve of the nineteen DEV cells are K–5 and share one cause: per-strand progression rows and grade deliverables are still TBD. Filling them moves most cells to CORE without new content.
  • 7 · Broaden cultural range in 6–8 Connect. Adding one non-Western or diasporic sampling tradition to US or the 6–8 Vanguard repertoire satisfies the world-cultures language directly.
  • 8 · Package a 9–12 offering. The content is there; the high-school syllabus, depth differentiation from grade 8, and semester-versus-year scoping are not.

Out of scope

  • Performance Music (MP) strand. Governs bands, choirs, and orchestras. If One City adds a performance ensemble, that strand needs its own pass, including the four extra performance indicators Wisconsin attaches to grades 3–5 and up.
  • National Core Arts Standards. Wisconsin's standards derive from NCAS (2014), so this mapping is a strong starting point, but the anchor-standard numbering differs and needs its own pass.
  • Per-student assessment evidence. This chart maps curriculum to indicators. Which artifact proves which indicator for a given student, and how that feeds One City report card categories, is still open.
