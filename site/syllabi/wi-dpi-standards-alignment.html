Wisconsin DPI Standards Alignment

All fifty-two K–8 General Music performance indicators, mapped to Koalacademy lesson codes, K–5 strands, and the discourse threads inside our lessons.

Status: second pass. Scoped to K–8, since One City ends at grade 8. Flags describe what is written or documented today, not what is planned for the pilot year. The maintained source of this chart is koalacademy-WI-standards-alignment.md in the curriculum repository.

What is being aligned

The Wisconsin Standards for Music organize music learning around four artistic processes — Create, Perform, Respond, and Connect. This program is aligned against three grade bands: K–2 e, 3–5 i, and 6–8 m. Fifty-two performance indicators in total. The 9–12 band is out of scope.

The alignment uses the General Music (MG) strand, which Wisconsin defines as formal courses exploring music in a non-performance setting — explicitly including music technology, composition, and improvisation. The Performance Music strand governs bands, choirs, and orchestras, and is not part of this program.

Koalacademy content assessed here: the fifty written 6–8 lessons, the six documented classroom games, the K–5 strand map, the nine grade syllabi, the Vanguard Songs framework, and the written-work stance.

How this program teaches history and culture

Cultural, historical, and industry context is taught as discourse inside the lesson that raises it — not as a separate history unit. The context is the conversation the material demands at the moment it comes up.

Teaching Timbaland's signature style in US.2 means teaching what audiences expected of a Timbaland record in 2001, how We Need a Resolution went past that expectation, how a single went viral before social media, and what the industry's “crossover” label assumed about audiences. Discussing the arrangement of the grade 8 anchor Forever YHWH means discussing Black gospel tradition and sacred vocal texture, because there is a choir in it. A separately titled unit on either subject would duplicate work the lesson already does.

So the chart measures where discourse actually lives. Threads that arise reliably are written into Discourse blocks in the curriculum, flagged EMBED below, and inventoried in the Connect discourse map.

How to read the chart

Every cell carries the Wisconsin indicator code, a short restatement of the indicator, the Koalacademy codes that deliver it, and exactly one coverage flag.

  • COREWritten material delivers the indicator today — a lesson, a documented game, or a documented framework.
  • EMBEDDelivered as documented discourse inside a lesson or a Vanguard sitting, rather than by a lesson named for it.
  • DEVPartially covered, or named as intent in a syllabus, with materials and pacing still to build.
  • OUTIntentionally deprioritized for this program, with a stated reason. Not a gap.
  • GAPNo coverage and no plan. Currently none.

Curriculum codes

  • BMT KSN US D4 SONGFORM SRP DYS AW AEP PERFORMANCE — Koalacademy lesson codes, grades 6–8.
  • BEAT PITCH TIMBRE FORM NOTATE LISTEN CREATE — K–5 strand codes.
  • GAMES — the documented game bank: Beat Freeze, Silent Ball, Timbre Scavenger, Form Detective, Balloon War Trivia, Musical Taboo.
  • VS — Vanguard Songs anchor listening.
  • JOURNAL — listening journals, exit tickets, and written checks.

Where the program is strong

  • The 6–8 project spine covers three standards at once. The DYS → AW → AEP → PERFORMANCE arc yields a composition, a rehearsed live performance, documented analysis and reflection, and cultural context from one body of student work — so standards evidence falls out of existing deliverables.
  • Technology-based creation is a direct hit. The 6–8 Create indicators name technology and alternative notation explicitly; Koala sequencing, chopping, pad mapping, and step editing satisfy them more cleanly than a traditional general music course would.
  • Analysis runs deep for middle school. Two full analysis passes over a student-chosen playlist plus five case studies go beyond what the 6–8 Respond indicators ask for.
  • Connect is carried by discourse, which is more durable than a history unit. Because context arrives attached to a record students are already studying, it lands as explanation rather than as a detour — and it recurs, since the same songs return all year.
  • Rights and ethics is enrichment beyond the standards. No MG indicator requires the SRP component, and it still strengthens the claim on MG4.Cn.9.m and Cn.12.m.
  • Vanguard Songs is the K–5 alignment engine. One anchor recording revisited through the active strand covers most of Respond and all of Connect with no new material.

Areas for further alignment

Ordered by standards impact per unit of work.

  • 1 · Write the Discourse blocks that currently live only in the teacher's head. US.2 and the grade 8 anchor Forever YHWH are documented as the reference pattern. The remaining case studies and locked anchors need the same treatment so the discourse transfers to another teacher and is defensible to an administrator. This is writing down what is already taught.
  • 2 · Finish scope and sequence. Twelve of the fourteen DEV cells are K–5 and share one cause: per-strand progression rows and grade deliverables are still TBD. Filling them moves most cells to CORE without new content, because the intent is already documented.
  • 3 · Lock the K–5 Vanguard repertoire. The four EMBED cells depend on it, and song selection is the moment to spread the repertoire across cultures and eras — which is what makes the where does this come from lens work.
  • 4 · Name improvisation as its own practice. The only indicator row that is weak at every band. A documented call-and-response game for K–5, and an improvisation pass in AEP.1 where students play a live figure over their own loop rather than only performing effects.
  • 5 · Decide the singing and technique stance. Either commit to a documented amount of singing and instrument technique, or state in the master syllabus that this program reads “sing or play” as pad and sampler performance. Both are defensible; the ambiguity is not.

Out of scope

  • The 9–12 band. One City ends at grade 8. The original Koalacademy course would support a high school offering, but no 9–12 syllabus exists and building one is not a current priority. If that changes, the h indicators can be added back as a fourth column.
  • Concert etiquette and formal performer and audience roles. Sharing, critique, and audience expectations for class presentations are already covered by PERFORMANCE.4 and K–5 share-outs. Formal etiquette instruction across a variety of venues belongs to a performance program; adding it here would be paperwork rather than teaching. Worth revisiting if a public showcase becomes part of grade 8.
  • Performance Music (MP) strand. Governs bands, choirs, and orchestras. If One City adds a performance ensemble, that strand needs its own pass.
  • National Core Arts Standards. Wisconsin's standards derive from NCAS (2014), so this mapping is a strong starting point, but the anchor-standard numbering differs and needs its own pass.
  • Per-student assessment evidence. This chart maps curriculum to indicators. Which artifact proves which indicator for a given student, and how that feeds One City report card categories, is still open.
